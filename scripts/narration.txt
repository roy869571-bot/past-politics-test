In December 1949, a new intelligence organization took shape in the young State of Israel.

It would become known as the Mossad.

Its mission was to gather intelligence beyond Israel’s borders, assess threats, and support national security decisions.

But intelligence work rarely looks like the movies.

It can mean studying fragments of information, building sources, checking reports, and connecting details that seem unrelated.

The agency’s history has also included covert operations—some widely reported, others still hidden from public view.

That secrecy has made the Mossad famous, controversial, and difficult to fully understand.

This is the story of an intelligence service operating where information is power—and where the most important action may be the one the public never sees.
